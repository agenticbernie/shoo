# Shoo Interview Guide and Study Protocols

- Version: 0.1
- Status: Accepted — Gate 9
- Owner role: User Research Lead / Business Analyst / Product Manager
- Dependencies: User Research Plan; Assumption Ledger; Product Metrics
- Assumptions: Sessions last 45–75 minutes and can use either a fixture or consented project
- Unresolved questions: Vietnamese/English moderation split and incentive amount
- Last decision: Ask about observed past behavior before presenting Shoo concepts
- Next action: Pilot with two internal participants, revise wording, then recruit external cohort

## Baseline interview — 45 minutes

1. Tell me about the last time you resumed coding work in a new session or agent.
2. What did you have to reconstruct, where did you look, and what was still missing?
3. Which tools did you use—files, Git, issue tracker, chat history, notes, another person?
4. What was the consequence of wrong or stale context?
5. Which project/session data would you never permit a tool to capture or send?
6. How do you decide a project decision is current and authoritative today?
7. When would durable, portable, user-owned memory matter enough to complete wallet/delegate setup?
8. What would cause you to uninstall or reject Shoo?

Do not lead with “shared memory,” Walrus or blockchain. Ask for frequency and a concrete recent incident before asking for opinions.

## Moderated usability — 60 to 75 minutes

- 5 min: consent, data boundaries and think-aloud instruction.
- 10 min: install/init/project-link task.
- 15 min: capture/checkpoint/complete in OpenCode.
- 15 min: resume in Codex and identify first useful action.
- 10 min: inspect citation and correct stale/unverified memory.
- 10 min: failure scenario—offline or unavailable durable layer.
- 10 min: delegate/revocation or export/deletion comprehension.

After each task ask: “What do you think happened?”, “Where is the data now?”, “What would you do next?” and confidence 1–5. Do not explain the UI until the task ends.

## Outcome debrief

- What did Shoo save you from repeating?
- What did it include that was unnecessary or miss that was critical?
- Which source or status did you trust least, and why?
- Did Manual/Walrus feel like ownership value, setup tax, or both?
- Would you use it on a real project tomorrow? What must change first?
- After a demonstrated successful continuation: who pays, for what outcome, and what alternative budget does it replace?

## Observation sheet

Record task success, time, error, assistance, incorrect mental model, trust hesitation, manual re-explanation, correction, source use, failure recovery and notable quote. Separate observed behavior, participant claim and researcher inference.

## Severity

- Critical: security/authority misunderstanding can expose data or create false current truth.
- High: core resume cannot complete without moderator help.
- Medium: recoverable delay or wrong mental model.
- Low: copy/visual friction without outcome impact.

Two critical or the same high issue in at least 3 participants blocks cohort expansion until retested.
